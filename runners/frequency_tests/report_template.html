Frequency Security Tests

The islanding workflow can limit, for every island it creates, the power the island exchanges with the rest of the system before the split, so that its frequency stays within its requirements after the split. This report explains what that frequency-security constraint computes, then tests it in three steps on the savnw and ACTIVSg500 cases: the numbers it computes before any optimization, its behaviour inside the optimization, and whether the islands it allows actually keep their frequency in dynamic simulations.

Runs of 2 Nov 2026 Page built — Simulator ANDES 2.0 OPF solver Ipopt

Numbers in the text are from these runs, which use each generator's own governor in the nadir limit (1.4) and the corrected hydro governor in ANDES (F9). Every table and figure is drawn from the result files in results/frequency_tests/ when the page is built. The first runs of 19 Oct 2026, with one steam governor for every unit and ANDES unmodified, are kept in results/frequency_tests_old_model_2026-10-19/. Section 1 defines every symbol where it first appears; section 1.7 collects them in one table.

Summary

The constraint is implemented correctly: it computes what its formulas say, and the optimization enforces it, apart from one bug. The nadir limit now uses each generator's own governor from the .dyr, and with that it tracks the simulated machines closely. The weak part left is what the model assumes the island loses. When the islands it allows are simulated, the limits are safe for an island with a mix of steam, gas and hydro units, about 40 % too generous for an island with only hydro units whose voltage rises at the split, and wrong in one direction when opening the boundary lowers the island's voltages. These runs also found and corrected a bug in ANDES's hydro governor model (F9), which had made the hydro zones look worse than they are.

Findings

F1

bug A boundary line that is already out of service breaks the model

If one of the lines chosen for opening is already out of service (br_status = 0), frequency.jl still counts it as a boundary line of the island, but PowerModels leaves it out of the model, so the constraint looks up a flow variable that does not exist (KeyError). The report function has the same problem. Section 4, fix in 6.1.

F2

changed Hydro units respond far more slowly than a steam governor, and the model now knows it

frequency.jl used to model every governor as a steam turbine. Hydro governors are deliberately slow at first, because the water in the pipe cannot be sped up quickly. It now reads each unit's governor from the .dyr (1.4). The ACTIVSg500 SPARTANB zone has only hydro units: its nadir limit falls from 62 MW to 39 MW, and that becomes the zone's limit in place of the 49 MW RoCoF limit. In the sweep version of the zone the model's frequency is now within 0.14 to 0.23 Hz of the simulation from 25 to 100 MW of import (0.41 to 1.26 Hz in the first runs, partly because of F9). Section 5.6 goes through the numbers. Also sections 5.3, 5.5, 6.2.

F3

model Opening the boundary changes the island's voltages, and its load with them

In savnw the boundary lines also supply reactive power to the north island (about 200 Mvar). When they open, its voltages drop by about 0.11 pu and its load falls by about 72 MW, because the simulated loads draw less power at lower voltage. The model assumes the island loses exactly its active import. As a result, imports are safe beyond their limit (59.5 Hz is reached at 99 MW against a 77 MW limit) but exports are not: 60.5 Hz is exceeded at 34 MW of export. With the constraint on, the heavily loaded north island avoids load shedding but overshoots to 60.89 Hz. In SPARTANB the voltage rises instead, the load grows by about 13 MW, and this is now the main gap: 59.5 Hz is reached at 26 MW against a 36 MW nadir limit, and with the constraint on the frequency falls to 59.43 Hz and UFLS sheds 30 MW. Sections 5.2, 5.3, 5.6, change in 6.3.

F4

model The governors deliver less steady-state response than the model counts

In the MIDLANDS zone the governors deliver about 218 pu of response against the 258 pu the model counts, so the frequency settles more than 0.3 Hz off at 111 MW of import, inside the 129 MW quasi-steady-state limit. The zone's RoCoF limit of 102 MW is tighter, so at its limit it settles at 59.72 Hz, inside the band. Its nadir limit is 8 % too generous (59.5 Hz at 105 MW against 113 MW), which the RoCoF limit also covers. Section 5.3.

F5

model The simulated loads do not respond to frequency, and islands without governors depend on that response

The model assumes the load drops 1 % for every 1 % drop of frequency (load damping D). The simulated loads do not depend on frequency at all. With governors present this matters little. The savnw left island has no governor, so D is all that would hold its frequency: its limit is 1 MW, and even at that import its frequency drifts until underfrequency relays shed load and its only unit trips. Section 5.5, change in 6.4.

F6

data The optimization can dispatch a unit below the minimum its governor model allows

To export only 50 MW, the savnw top island needs unit 3011 at about 150 MW, but its governor model cannot go below 300 MW (30 % of its 1000 MVA rating). The optimization allows 0 MW. The simulation starts out of balance and the unit trips at 61.8 Hz. With the load at bus 3005 raised to 400 MW (as in islanding.jl) the same island is secure. Section 5.5, change in 6.5.

F7

note The constraint costs no solve time; its cost is preemptive load shedding

Solve times with and without it are the same. When an island cannot meet its limit by redispatch, the optimization sheds load before the split instead: 399 MW in SPARTANB, 78 MW in MIDLANDS, 389 MW outside the zone for YORK, 1,328 MW in GREENVIL (a zone with 12 MW of generation). Section 4.

F8

fixed The batch runner called run_islanding with a keyword it no longer has

batch_islanding.jl passed run_psse=, but run_islanding now takes run_dynamic_sim, so every batch run failed. Changed to run_dynamic_sim=run_psse.

F9

fixed ANDES 2.0's hydro governor model had scaling errors

ANDES keeps HYGOV's gate and flow on the system base. With S = machine MVA / 100, that leaves three errors on the machine base: the integrator gain is 1/S instead of 1/(r·Tr) (ANDES computes r·Tr but never uses it), Tw acts as Tw·S, and Dturb is S times too large. Hydro units here range from about 1 to 725 MVA, so the errors differ from unit to unit. Given the same speed, unmodified units were up to 8.8 times off the PSS®E block diagram; correct_hygov in andes_scripts/dynamic.py now brings them to a ratio of 1.00, except a few units of about 5 MVA or less, where ANDES's rate limit logic differs. This was checked against the block diagram as coded, not against a PSS®E run. The effect: SPARTANB reaches 59.5 Hz at 26 MW instead of 18 MW and no longer collapses at 150 MW; MIDLANDS settles closer to 60 Hz and becomes secure at its limit. TGOV1 and GAST do not have this problem. Sections 5.3, 5.6.

1What the frequency constraint does

This section explains the quantities the rest of the report tests. The code is runners/frequency.jl, called by run_islanding when frequency_security=true.

1.1The island and its scheduled import

The islanding workflow picks a set of boundary lines to open (the target lines, from bus pairs or from a zone boundary) and solves an optimal power flow (OPF) for the operating point just before they open. Opening them splits the network into islands. Before the split, each island exchanges power with the rest of the system over those lines.

The scheduled import ΔP of an island is the net active power flowing into it over its boundary lines in that operating point, measured at the island end of each line (so losses on the boundary lines count toward it). When the lines open, the island loses ΔP at once. An island that imported (ΔP > 0) is then short of generation: a deficit, and its frequency falls. An island that exported (ΔP < 0) has more generation than load: a surplus, and its frequency rises. The frequency constraint limits |ΔP| for every island.

1.4The SFR model behind the limits

frequency.jl gives each island one frequency, the idea of the system frequency response (SFR) model of Anderson and Mirheydar (1990), and gives each online unit its own governor from the .dyr. With Δf the frequency deviation in per unit (Δf = (f − 60)/60) and t = 0 the moment the lines open:

2H · dΔf/dt = Σi ΔPm,i − D · Δf − ΔP
ΔPm,i = Gi · ( (1 + wi) · qi − wi · xi ) − di · Δf
Ts,i · dzi/dt = Δf − zi,   T1,i · dxi/dt = −( si · Δf + (1 − si) · zi ) − xi,   T2,i · dqi/dt = xi − qi
  • First line, the swing equation. The rotating mass (inertia H) speeds up or slows down by the power imbalance: the extra mechanical power from all the governors, minus the change of load with frequency D·Δf, minus the lost import ΔP.
  • Second and third lines, each unit's governor and turbine. A held frequency drop ends at gain Gi. The fast share si of that follows the frequency at once and the rest comes through the slow lag Ts,i; the sum then passes two short lags, T1,i and T2,i. For a hydro unit wi gives the water column's wrong-way start and di its turbine damping.
  • Numbers from the .dyr. Table below, each gain scaled by MBASEi/100 (calc_governor_unit). For HYGOV this is the governor's exact linear form, gate/speed = (1/R)(1 + s·Tr)/(1 + s·Tr(r + R)/R), and in a single-unit test it stays within 1.5 % of the full block diagram's nadir limit on all 40 units. The gate opening g = Pg/(At·MBASE) + qNL comes from the case's dispatch before the solve. Governor limits (maximum and minimum output, rate limits) and deadbands are not modelled, so the response is linear: twice the deficit gives twice the deviation.
  • RoCoF limit. At t = 0 nothing has responded yet (Δf = 0, ΔPm = 0), so dΔf/dt = −ΔP/(2H). In Hz/s that is 60·ΔP/(2H); keeping it within 0.5 Hz/s gives |ΔP| ≤ 2H·0.5/60.
  • QSS limit. Once everything has settled, each unit gives its full response. The limit counts K = Σ 1/R (1.5), so Δf = −ΔP/(D + K), and keeping it within 0.3/60 pu gives |ΔP| ≤ (D + K)·0.3/60. For HYGOV the true final gain is At/R, about 1.2 times larger, so this errs on the safe side.
  • Nadir limit. There is no simple formula, so frequency.jl simulates the model: forward Euler integration with a 0.01 s step over 20 s (the simulated time after the split in dynamic.py). It finds by bisection the largest ΔP whose lowest frequency stays at or above 59.5 Hz, to 10−5 pu (0.001 MW).

1.5Where H, K and D come from

Once per island, before the optimization (add_frequency_security!), from the online units and loads inside the island. Values are in per unit (pu) on the system base: power divided by 100 MVA (the base of both cases), frequency deviation divided by 60 Hz. So K = 200 pu means the island's governors add 200 × 100 MW for a 1 pu (60 Hz) drop, which is 333 MW per Hz.

An island without any unit (no inertia) gets no limit. It cannot survive the split anyway: the simulation de-energizes it. Its load, though, was supplied over boundary lines from a neighbouring island, so that supply is part of the neighbour's scheduled import.

1.6The constraint in the optimization

build_custom_pf (src/prob/opf.jl) calls constraint_island_frequency_security when the network data carries the islands. For each island it adds one linear constraint on the OPF's flow variables:

−L ≤ Σlines l −pl,i,j ≤ L

The sum runs over the island's boundary lines; i is the line's bus inside the island and j the bus outside, so pl,i,j is the power leaving the island at that end and the sum of −p is the scheduled import. It is the same in the AC and DC models.

3Test 1: the offline limits

Before any optimization, frequency.jl reads the machine data, finds the islands and computes their limits. Test 1 recomputes each of these independently and compares (1_offline.jl), for every scenario of section 2.

3.1Machine data from the .dyr

read_dyr_machine_data reads Hi from each unit's generator model, and Ri and the speed numbers of 1.4 from its governor model, by parameter position. ANDES has its own .dyr reader; it stores the inertia as M = 2H on the unit's MVA base and the droop R on the same base. The test reads both cases with ANDES (andes_dyr_check.py) and compares, unit by unit, H, R and the MVA base. The speed numbers' positions were checked once against ANDES's reader table.

All online units, Julia against ANDES

Result: identical for every unit. savnw unit 3018 has no governor record in its .dyr, so it adds inertia but no governor gain.

3.2Islands, H, K, D and the RoCoF and QSS limits

Eight checks on every island the split leaves:

  • the islands together contain every bus exactly once;
  • no in-service line other than the target lines joins two islands;
  • the island's boundary lines are exactly the target lines with one end inside it, each with its inside bus first;
  • H, K and D equal a separate hand calculation (1.5);
  • the RoCoF and QSS limits equal their formulas (1.3).

Columns: island load; online units (with a governor); H, K, D on the 100 MVA base; the three limits in MW; which limit is the smallest; checks passed.

3.3The nadir limit

Four checks on the bisection that gives the nadir limit, for every island:

  • At the limit, the model's lowest frequency is 59.5 Hz, and a step 2×10−5 pu (0.002 MW) larger goes below it.
  • Linearity: the nadir deviation per MW of deficit is the same at 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 times the limit, as the model has no limits in it. The spread is reported.
  • Integration accuracy: the limit recomputed with the exact solution of the same linear equations (matrix exponential, 1 ms steps) instead of forward Euler at 0.01 s.
  • Window: whether the lowest point falls inside the 20 s window, or the frequency is still falling at 20 s.

Columns: nadir limit; model frequency at the limit and just above it; linearity spread (relative); limit from the exact solution; difference of the code's limit from it; time of the lowest point; whether it lies inside the 20 s window.

Result: the bisection is exact, the model is linear to rounding error, and Euler integration makes the limit at most 0.40 % smaller than exact (on the safe side). The lowest point comes 0.5–2.3 s after the split in every island with governors (0.5 s in GREENVIL, which has almost no inertia). Only the islands without a governor have no turning point within 20 s, which makes their nadir limit depend on the window length.

3.4Which limit binds

The RoCoF limit depends only on H, the QSS limit only on D and K, and the nadir limit on all three. Figure 4 scales one of them at a time for three islands and shows how the three limits move; the map scales H and K together and colours each point by the limit that is smallest.

Result: RoCoF is the smallest limit in 19 of the 31 islands and the nadir limit in 10; the other two have no governor, where QSS is smallest. With one steam governor for every unit, RoCoF was the smallest in 29. The nadir limit takes over where the governors are slow relative to the inertia, as in SPARTANB and the savnw bottom island.

4Test 2: the constraint in the optimization

Each test solves one scenario twice, with frequency security off and on, and checks one expected outcome (2_model.jl):

  • binds: off, some island's import exceeds its limit; on, every import is within its limit and at least one is at it (within 0.05 MW).
  • within: the same, without requiring an import at its limit. Used where the objective is flat: in the DC model with equal costs there are no losses, every dispatch costs the same, and the solver may stop anywhere inside the limit.
  • unchanged: off, every import is already within its limit; on, the solution must be the same (objective within 10−6, imports within 0.001 MW).
  • infeasible: on, the solver finds no solution, and a hand check agrees that none exists without shedding. The hand check gives each island the import range its own units allow (load − maximum output to load − minimum output), cuts it to [−L, L], and requires the imports of all islands to add up to minus the load of the dead islands (whose supply came over the boundary).

Columns: expected outcome and result; solver status with the constraint on; for each island its limit (and which one) and its import off → on; preemptive shedding off → on; Ipopt solve time off / on.

Result: 22 of 23 tests pass. The constraint holds exports and imports at their limits, leaves the solution untouched when the limits are not reached (including the islanding.jl SPARTANB setup, still 407.4 MW of shedding), and makes the problem infeasible exactly when the hand check says the limits cannot be met. Solve times are the same with and without it; only the infeasible cases take longer, because Ipopt has to prove that no solution exists.

5Test 3: dynamic simulation in ANDES

Tests 1 and 2 show the constraint does what its formulas say. Test 3 checks whether the formulas describe what actually happens, by simulating the islands after the split (3_dynamic.jl).

5.1How the runs work

Every run exports the OPF solution as a PSS®E RAW file and runs andes_scripts/dynamic.py, as run_islanding does: ANDES solves the power flow of the exported case, opens the target lines at t = 5 s and simulates until 25 s, so 20 s after the split. dynamic.py converts every load the way the PSS®E script does: active power as constant current (P proportional to the voltage) and reactive power as constant admittance (Q proportional to the voltage squared). Neither depends on frequency, so the simulation has no load damping (D = 0). It also corrects ANDES's HYGOV after setup (correct_hygov, F9).

Two kinds of runs:

  • Calibration sweeps fix the scheduled import of one island at a series of values and simulate each. The import is fixed by an equality constraint added in the test only (the OPF otherwise as opf). The FRQTPAT and LDSHBL relays are removed from the .dyr, so the frequency shows what the system does on its own, without relays cutting load or units. Three sweeps: savnw north-south (−200 to +200 MW into the north island), ACTIVSg500 MIDLANDS with loads ×0.7 (−150 to +400 MW), SPARTANB with loads ×0.5 (−75 to +300 MW). Each run also gives the response of the other island, which receives about the opposite import.
  • Off and on runs go through run_islanding unchanged with the case's own .dyr, relays included, frequency security off and then on, and record what the relays do.

What is measured in each run (analyze_andes.py):

5.2Opening the boundary also changes voltages, and the load follows

The model assumes the island loses exactly its scheduled import. Figure 6 compares that with the power step its machines actually take up. For the ACTIVSg500 zones the slope agrees within 3 %, though SPARTANB's machines also take up about 13 MW at zero import (5.6). In savnw the two do not agree: deficits come out smaller and surpluses larger, and at zero import the north island still takes a 72 MW surplus.

Figure 7 shows why. The boundary lines also carry reactive power into the islands (part of it their own line charging). In savnw the north island receives 156–249 Mvar before the split; when the lines open it loses that support and its load-bus voltages drop by 0.08–0.12 pu at once. The simulated loads draw active power in proportion to the voltage, so the north island's load falls by about 72 MW and the south island's by about 160 MW. The ACTIVSg500 zones exchange less reactive power over their boundaries and their voltages move by at most 0.03 pu; the large rest-of-system islands lose 20–90 MW of load, little compared with their size.

5.6The hydro-only zone SPARTANB, step by step

This is the case behind findings F2, F3 and F9. With the model's governors now right, it shows what is left.

What the constraint is meant to do here

SPARTANB has 8 generating units, all hydro (water turbines), with about 608 MW of generation, and 1,043 MW of load. It normally imports power from the rest of the grid over its 18 boundary lines. When those lines open, the imported power disappears at once. The zone is then short of power by about the amount it was importing, and its frequency starts to fall. The constraint caps the import so that the fall stays acceptable.

It computes three caps and applies the smallest. For the sweep version of the zone (loads halved, section 5.1) they are 49 MW for the speed of the fall (RoCoF), 36 MW for the lowest frequency (the nadir) and 75 MW for the frequency it settles at. The zone as it is in the off/on run has 49, 39 and 78 MW. So the constraint lets this zone import up to 39 MW, and the nadir sets it. With one steam governor for every unit the nadir cap was 62 MW and the limit was RoCoF's 49 MW.

The governors: slow on purpose

A governor reacts to a frequency drop by letting more steam or water into the turbine. How strongly it reacts is set by its droop, and a smaller droop means a stronger reaction. A hydro governor has two. The permanent droop R (5 % for most units here) applies once everything has settled. On top of it sits a temporary droop r that applies at first and fades away. The reason is the water. A long column of water cannot be sped up quickly, so if the gate is opened fast the turbine's power first dips and the control becomes unstable. The temporary droop makes the governor open the gate gently at first.

In SPARTANB the three large units (236 MVA each) carry 97 % of the zone's governor response. Their temporary droop is 0.11, 0.47 and 0.29, so the fast part of their response is 31 %, 10 % and 15 % of the full one, and the rest arrives with time constants of 4.7, 18 and 30 s. The nadir is decided in the first 2 to 3 s, so most of that slow part comes too late for it. On top of that, the water starting time of 0.3 to 0.8 s makes each unit's power dip briefly before it rises. The model now has all of this (1.4).

6What to change

In order of importance. 6.1 is a bug fix; 6.2 is done in these runs; 6.3 to 6.5 change the model behind the limits; 6.6 uses the sweep results directly.

6.1Skip out-of-service lines when building the island's boundary

calc_island_boundary_arcs lists every target line with one end in the island, including lines with br_status = 0. Such a line carries no flow, and PowerModels leaves it out of the model, so constraint_island_frequency_security fails when it looks up its flow variable, and print_frequency_security fails the same way on the solution. A guard that skips lines out of service in calc_island_boundary_arcs fixes both. The islands themselves need no change: they are computed on the network with the target lines opened, where an already-open line makes no difference.

6.2Give the model each island's own governor dynamics (done)

frequency.jl gave every island the same governor, a reheat steam turbine with a fast share of 0.3 and a slow lag of 7 s. It now gives each unit its own (1.4), still with one linear cap per island found by bisection:

  • Steam units (TGOV1). Fast share T2/T3 and slow lag T3 are close to the old constants, except savnw units 101 and 102 (T2 = T3 = 1 s: no reheat delay). That is why the savnw south island's nadir limit rises from 277 to 403 MW; RoCoF still sets its limit.
  • Hydro units (HYGOV). These changed the most: the SPARTANB nadir limit falls from 62 to 39 MW, YORK's from 220 to 147 MW, the savnw bottom island's from 136 to 98 MW.
  • Gas units (GAST) are much faster than a reheat steam turbine. In MIDLANDS, where 4 of the 11 online units are gas, the nadir limit rises from 102 to 113 MW; RoCoF still sets its limit.

No island's final limit got looser in the 31 islands tested, and the sweeps (5.3) confirm the change: the model's frequency is within 0.14 to 0.23 Hz of the simulation for SPARTANB from 25 to 100 MW, and within 0.08 Hz for MIDLANDS up to 150 MW. Governor limits are still not modelled (6.5).

6.3Account for the voltage change at the split

This is now the largest error left. The model assumes the island loses exactly its active import. In savnw the island also loses the reactive power the boundary lines supplied, its voltages drop by about 0.1 pu, and its load, which depends on voltage, falls by 70–160 MW (5.2). This makes deficits smaller and surpluses larger than modelled, so the symmetric limit is conservative for imports and unsafe for exports. In SPARTANB the voltage rises by about 0.03 pu and the load grows by about 13 MW, so the limit is unsafe for imports (5.6). Two options:

  • Add the expected load change to the scheduled import before applying the limit: with constant-current loads, ΔPload ≈ Σ Pload × ΔV, where ΔV is each load bus's voltage change at the split. predict_voltage_change_from_opened_lines in islanding_core.jl already predicts ΔV from the network Jacobian. That makes the effective imbalance, and the limit, depend on the direction of the import.
  • Or limit the reactive import over the boundary as well, so the islands keep their voltage when it opens. This also relates to the voltage part of the proposal.

6.4Use the load damping the simulated loads actually have

frequency.jl assumes the load falls 1 % per 1 % of frequency (LOAD_DAMPING = 1.0). The loads in dynamic.py (constant current and admittance) do not depend on frequency, so LOAD_DAMPING = 0 matches the simulation; for islands with governors it lowers the nadir limit by 5 to 18 % in the zones checked (MIDLANDS with loads ×0.7, SPARTANB; the green dashed curves in Figure 8). For an island without any governor (K = 0) the QSS limit then becomes zero and the nadir limit tiny: such an island cannot be made frequency-secure, as the savnw left island shows (5.5). It should be reported as such rather than given a 1 MW limit.

6.5Keep the dispatch inside the governor limits

The OPF uses each unit's pmin and pmax from the RAW file; the governor models have their own limits (TGOV1 and GAST VMIN and VMAX, HYGOV GMIN and GMAX, in pu of MBASE). In savnw every pmin is 0 while TGOV1 VMIN is 0.3 of MBASE, so the OPF can dispatch a unit to an output its governor model cannot hold (5.5, top island). Raising pmin to VMIN × MBASE before the solve avoids that. The same governor limits also cap the upward response: a unit dispatched near VMAX × MBASE gives little response in reality. Now that the nadir model has one response per unit, the OPF can be made to keep the headroom each unit's response needs.

6.6Use the sweeps as the proposal's outer loop

The proposal describes an outer loop: simulate the solution, tighten the limit where the simulation is worse than expected, solve again. The sweeps give the tightening directly: the ratios in the table of 5.3. For the islands here: the nadir limit of the hydro-only zone by 0.72, the QSS limit of MIDLANDS by 0.86, savnw north's export limit by 0.44. Applying those factors to L and solving again would make those islands secure under the same simulation; changes 6.3 and 6.4 would make the factors close to 1 in the first place.
