Frequency security for fixed-boundary islanding: short summary

savnw and ACTIVSg500 · simulations in ANDES 2.0 · 19 Oct 2026 · figures fig1 to fig3 in this folder

The constraint

When the boundary lines open, each island suddenly loses the power ΔP it was importing over them (or gains it, if exporting), and its frequency moves. The constraint caps |ΔP| before the split so that the frequency changes no faster than 0.5 Hz/s (RoCoF), stays above 59.5 Hz (the first load-shedding stage) and settles within 0.3 Hz of 60 Hz. With the inertia H = Σ Hi · MBASEi / 100, the governor strength K = Σ (MBASEi / 100) / Ri and the load damping D = 1.0 × island load (Hi and the droop Ri from the .dyr, MBASEi the MVA rating), the RoCoF and settling caps are 2H · 0.5 / 60 and (D + K) · 0.3 / 60. For the nadir, the island is one machine with one steam governor, stepped forward with Euler (h = 0.01 s, 20 s, from zero):

ΔPm,k = −K · ( FH · Δfk + (1 − FH) · zk )
Δfk+1 = Δfk + h · ( ΔPm,k − D · Δfk − ΔP ) / (2H)
zk+1 = zk + h · ( Δfk − zk ) / TR

Δf is the frequency deviation in pu, ΔPm the governors' extra power, FH = 0.3 the share of turbine power that responds at once and z the rest, delayed by TR = 7 s. The nadir is 60 · (1 + mink Δfk) Hz, and a bisection finds the largest ΔP that keeps it at or above 59.5 Hz. The OPF then enforces −L ≤ ΔP ≤ L, with L the smallest cap.

What we found

The caps are computed correctly, and the OPF enforces them as intended (Fig. 1), shedding load before the split when redispatch is not enough; the only problem is a bug when a boundary line is already out of service. In simulation the constraint always helps but is not always enough (Fig. 2): without it every scenario ends in load shedding, a trip or a runaway frequency, and with it only a few become fully secure. The main reason is the nadir model (Fig. 3). It is right for zones with steam and gas units, but too optimistic for hydro units, which respond slowly at first. In savnw, opening the lines also drops the voltage and the load, which the model ignores.

Not covered, and next

flow_shed was not used to test the constraint: it already pushes the boundary flows toward zero and would hide the constraint's effect. All simulations are in ANDES, none in PSS®E.
